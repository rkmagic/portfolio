## Context

15-09-2026

A self case study of how I use AI for product discovery. I have tried many tools, but I usually rely on the 2-3 that help most. Not everything below is product-building only. Some of it is personal productivity that also extends to product management.

## Basic Process

The prompt and the model selection are a cautious choice before starting work. Typically, I follow the 4-step [framework](https://yourstory.com/2025/02/greg-brockman-perfect-ai-prompt-framework) Greg Brockman (OpenAI President) once shared. A role, input format, output format and context dump.

For models - mostly I rely on Sonnet in Claude and Opus if it requires deeper thinking with data analysis or challenging my thoughts. I barely touch the effort toggle and it's usually set to high. Power of defaults I guess.

## Evidence

### Research

Research is a large part of my AI usage. On desktop I barely use Google Search beyond Translate or Maps. For latest information I mostly use Perplexity, which I find better than Claude at collecting recent sources. Last week, when I asked about recent Bending Spoons acquisitions, Claude said Airtable was the most recent. I was sure that was wrong. Perplexity gave the correct answer. I have seen this pattern often.

For deeper research I use Claude Research to synthesize a report. That helped when I evaluated the Apple Music app and needed to analyze reviews. Claude returned a top-10 theme list with user counts under each theme. I still manually checked a few reviews to catch hallucinations. Combining Perplexity and Claude for competitive or market research is a strong base for product decisions, and you can build a skill to compile reports to your needs.

Some people downplay Perplexity. It is still a strong research engine.

Preparing customer-interview questionnaires is another favorite use case. I give the themes I want to cover, relevant files for context, info about the speaker, time duration, and interview goal. Claude sometimes suggests questions I missed. Recently, while preparing to interview someone who built a lot of AI automation, my draft covered many topics but not human-in-the-loop decisions. Claude suggested it, and that was an aha moment.

### Data Analysis

This is the standout use case for me. AI can process in seconds what used to take an analyst hours. I have not worked with customer data in the last year during my MBA, but at work AI mainly helped with SQL. We ran analytics on Apache Superset, so analysis meant writing SQL, and Claude helped. Natural-language querying is now more common, including Databricks with MCP connectors.

Recent example: in a data fluency course we analyzed a life-expectancy dataset across 193 countries and built a Looker Studio dashboard. Before analysis I used Claude to find anomalies, since bad data warps inferences. Claude flagged countries mislabeled as developing/developed, and that China's population was unusually low in some years.

### PowerPoint creation

Claude PowerPoints were useful early on. I drafted the outline, gave Claude context, and it generated the slides. One example was a financial accounting case: I worked the Excel, Claude built the PowerPoint. Over time the decks started looking the same. I tried Gamma.ai, Beautiful.ai, and similar tools, but the sameness was still obvious. Then I used Gemini to generate a slide-design image and recreated it manually in PowerPoint. That looked much better.

I have not yet found a tool that reliably produces consulting or investment-banking grade decks packed with dense information.

![powerpoint_image](/images/powerpoint.png)

This was me being picky about repeated templates, colors, and fonts, but it can become an easy pipeline with a skill that also follows brand guidelines. Example pipeline:

User wants a PowerPoint → ask for outline → discuss until the outline is solid → finalize outline → create 50% of slides → get feedback → finish the rest.

### Knowledge Base

Something I intend to build more robustly: compress MBA documents, meeting notes, articles, projects, PDFs, and PowerPoints into a single knowledge base I can talk to.

I recently built a smaller version in Cursor for a course: a knowledge base for a publicly traded company. I chose Delta Air Lines out of interest in aviation. Process and report: [rkmagic/kb](https://github.com/rkmagic/kb-delta-analytics). The knowledge base visualizes easily in tools like Obsidian. Snapshot below.

![obsidian-graph](/images/obsidian.png)

The same process works for product docs: a knowledge base of `.md` files covering user interviews, meeting notes, strategy docs, PRDs, and more. Work becomes a living document, and new artefacts can be created and challenged with enough context.

## Reflection

Takeaways:
- See opportunities for AI usage
- Use it to challenge your thinking
- Build or use skills for repeat work, and update them when needed
- Humans still need to orchestrate the work to avoid generic output
- Verify the output. No matter how good the model is, it can miss context
