## Context

Built for my final project in the AI for Business MBA course, May 2026.

## Problem

I received receipts and bank alerts by email across different cards, and wanted one private place with a full view of my spend. I also kept forgetting about subscriptions I no longer needed, then losing money to auto-renewal.

### Persona

The persona I built for is the cross-border professional. They move between countries and pay with whatever works locally: a home credit card, a forex card, a local wallet. Their spend is split across rails that never roll up into one view. On top of that sit a dozen app and SaaS subscriptions on their own renewal dates and their own currencies. This person is not trying to budget to the cent. They want to answer "where did my money go last month?" without exporting three statements into a spreadsheet.

### Product principles

1. **Fact-based answers only.** Every insight, number, and chat answer traces back to a transaction in the database. When the data does not support an answer, the product says so instead of filling the gap with a generic finance tip.
2. **Deterministic first, AI where it earns its place.** Rules handle parsing and database writes. AI is used where rules break down - unfamiliar merchants, custom HTML receipts - and its output passes a gate before anything is written.
3. **Separate sections for easier viewability.** Overview, categories, currencies, and subscriptions each answer one question, so nothing has to be reassembled across views.
4. **My data stays in my own stack.** No bank credentials handed to an aggregator, no third party holding the ledger.

### Success criteria

1. **Coverage** - the share of spend emails that reach the database correctly, with no manual correction.
2. **Categorization quality** - merchants resolved to the right category, tracked by the confidence score, with "Others" as the failure bucket to keep small.
3. **Grounded chat** - no answer that cites a number absent from the last 90 days of transactions.
4. **Subscription recall** - every active subscription shows the correct cadence and next renewal date before it charges.

## Process

The solution needed to be end-to-end: ingest the data, write it to a database, and surface it in a dashboard.

Getting n8n outputs into the right formats took time. Even with a robust prompt, Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 4.6 sometimes failed to follow it fully, so I added code-snippet guardrails. I spotted failures in the n8n traces, which resulted in these deterministic blocks. In a production system, observability tools like LangSmith or MLflow would make this easier.

![evals-fin](/images/evals_fintrack.png)

I evaluated both models on ~40 transaction emails covering subscriptions, Amazon orders, bank alerts, promotions, and marketing. Sonnet 4.6 extracted every transaction and categorized 95% of them correctly, and it called tools when it should. Haiku 4.5 ran at roughly a third of the cost and twice the speed, but landed at 90% extraction and 65% categorization, and rarely reached for a tool. In a ledger a wrong category costs more than a slow one, so extraction runs on Sonnet 4.6. It cleared the cases I expected to be hard - Amazon shipped and dispatched mails, payment reminders, trial and cancelled subscriptions. It still misses on PDF-only emails, some bank alerts, and unusual merchant names, which is what the SerpAPI lookup and the "Others" bucket absorb. Forty emails is a small sample, so these are directional, not guarantees. I also manually verified a few chatbot conversations by back-calculating the numbers, to confirm the answers were grounded in the data rather than invented.

The dashboard offers:
- Transaction history trends over calendar days and weeks.
- Categorization by type (food, travel, groceries, etc.) and by merchant.
- Spend by currency using exchange rates.
- AI insights and a chatbot over the last 90 days of transactions.
- Subscription tracking with renewal dates and cadence (weekly / monthly / yearly).

### What the n8n automation does
- Connects to Gmail to extract spend from text, PDFs, or HTML (some companies render custom HTML in emails).
- Uses rule-based logic wherever possible. Database writes are risky, so AI output is verified with gates like IF nodes in n8n.
- Supports updates to existing subscriptions or transactions.
- Uses an AI agent with SerpAPI to Google-search ambiguous merchant names for better categorization. Unresolved merchants go to "Others". Categorization includes a confidence score that can be tuned.
- Resolves and cleans canonical names (e.g. Spotify AB, or tags like Anthropic, PBC).

**Stack:** n8n, Claude, SerpAPI, Supabase, Next.js, Vercel

### Don't apps like Apple Pay / Revolut already do this?

They do a version of this on their own rails: statements, some categories, and a filter box. That falls short for questions like "how much did I spend on coffee this quarter?" Other SaaS tools exist for subscriptions, but this dashboard is customized to my preferences. Sometimes people use a different payment option like a forex card, for example, and visibility disappears. This workflow also researches merchants that are unfamiliar.

Fintrack is built around that: a ledger from the emails I already receive, plus a chatbot grounded in the last 90 days of *those* transactions, not generic finance answers.

## Future Work

- The system assumes transactions land in email, so alerts must be configured for that. Where APIs exist, they can be ingested instead. SMS could also work when email alerts are unavailable. This data is highly sensitive, so handling needs care.
- Calendar alerts for subscription renewals via Google Calendar API.
- The dashboard is currently grounded on the last 90 days. That can extend on demand by pulling more data.
- A mobile app could capture location at transaction time to better identify merchants (e.g. a cafeteria inside a university building).
- The n8n workflow can be hosted in the cloud.
