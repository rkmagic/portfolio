> **TL;DR** — DAZN spends billions on broadcasting rights, but the app treats every fan as a generic sports browser. The homepage ignores my team, live games load late, and the player makes me answer a question before I can watch. Cancelling means arguing with a chatbot. The content is the moat. Right now the product is the leak. I'm an avid football fan, and DAZN can improve a few flows to make streaming feel seamless.

My opinion only.

## Company background

DAZN (pronounced "da zone") launched in 2016 out of Perform Group, a UK sports-data and media business owned by Len Blavatnik's Access Industries. The first markets were Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Japan, and the pitch was simple: a "Netflix for sports", with live matches over the internet and no cable bundle.

Since then DAZN has grown into one of the largest sports-only streamers in the world. It is available in 200+ markets, and the rights it holds change by country. Examples include Serie A in Italy, LaLiga in Spain, the Bundesliga and Champions League packages in Germany, Matchroom boxing globally, NFL Game Pass internationally, and the global rights to the 2025 FIFA Club World Cup.

The strategy is to buy premium live rights, become the default place fans go for them, and build more revenue on top of the subscription: betting, ticketing, merchandise and fan engagement. It is a capital-heavy bet. In 2024 alone DAZN spent about **$3 billion on media rights** against roughly $3.2 billion in revenue, with close to **$10 billion** still committed over the coming years ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/financial/dazn-cuts-losses-by-almost-a-third-in-2024-as-revenue-grows-once-again/)). Live sport is watched in spikes, so fans sign up for a season and leave when it ends. With close to **20 million paying subscribers** ([DAZN](https://dazngroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/DAZN_Half_Year_Review_2024_JP_15_SEPT-5.pdf)), every friction point in that loop is expensive.

## Product and business model

- **Subscription tiers** that differ by market, usually monthly vs annual commitment and sometimes a "Standard" vs "Unlimited" split.
- **Pay-per-view** on top of the subscription for big boxing events.
- **Adjacent revenue:** DAZN Bet in some markets, merchandise, and FanZone (live chat, polls and predictions during games) as an engagement layer.
- **Platforms:** web, iOS, Android, smart TVs, consoles and streaming sticks.

That model means DAZN's economics depend on two things: **retention between big events** and **engagement during them**. Both come down to how quickly a fan gets from opening the app to watching their game.

## Who it is for

The core user is not a "sports fan" in the abstract. It is someone who follows **one team, one league, or one sport** and subscribes because of it. Their jobs to be done are narrow and urgent:

1. "My team is playing, get me into the live game now."
2. "I missed it, show me highlights or a replay of my team."
3. "When is the next game?"

Everything else is secondary. That is the lens for this teardown. I am that user.

## What works

- **Rights portfolio.** When DAZN has the league you care about, there is often no legal alternative. That is real leverage.
- **Multi-sport breadth.** For casual browsing on a weekend, the catalogue has depth across football, boxing, NFL, motorsport and niche sports.
- **Device coverage.** It works across TV, mobile and web, and the stream quality is generally solid once playback starts. One subscription covers all devices and platforms.
- **Intent behind FanZone.** Social watching is a good idea for live sport. Watching alone is less sticky than watching with a crowd.

### 1. The homepage is not personalized to my team

**What happens:** I cannot set my team so that my sport and my matches lead the homepage. When my team is playing, the home rails still push other sports and other games.

![DAZN homepage: MotoGP and DAZN Bet chips, a generic LaLiga match strip, and "Best of Fútbol" highlights, with nothing tied to my team](/images/teardowns/dazn/home.jpg)

*My team is Manchester United and there is a game live — and I can't see it when I open the app.*

**Why it matters:** The single most valuable moment in a sports app is "my team is live", and the homepage hides it behind generic editorial. Every extra scroll at kickoff is a moment of doubt about whether I am paying for the right thing. When a game is live, I'm filled with buzz and excitement, looking to get into the match as fast as I can — but DAZN makes me wait.

This is not a niche complaint. In a [WSC Sports survey of 1,050 US fans](https://wsc-sports.com/blog/industry-insights/making-every-interaction-count-the-commercial-impact-of-personalization/), more than half had cancelled or switched a streamer because of poor personalization. Dyn, a German sports streamer, asked new subscribers one question — favorite sport — and built home around the answer. Retention jumped **35%**.

**Likely reason:** Home is programmed editorially per market to promote the whole catalogue, and possibly to push rights DAZN has paid a lot for. Personalization would compete with that. If DAZN can't promise viewership on a property, they risk losing the rights next cycle.

### 2. Live games take 3–5 seconds to appear

**What happens:** On launch, the page shows stale or placeholder content for several seconds before the live games load.

**Why it matters:** Latency hurts most right before kickoff, and that is exactly when people open the app. A page that looks empty or out of date makes me think the stream is not live yet, or that something is broken. A few seconds sounds small, but fans notice. In a [Stats Perform survey of 1,000 NFL fans](https://www.statsperform.com/insights/super-bowl-live-streaming-experience/), **26%** said delays under five seconds already frustrate them, and **83%** said they would likely switch platforms if their stream felt delayed.

**Likely reason:** Live status is fetched after the page shell renders, with no cached or optimistic state and no clear loading state for the live rail.

### 3. A confirmation dialog before a live game

**What happens:** When I open a game that is live, I have to choose "start from beginning" or "go live" before playback starts.

**Why it matters:** For a live match the overwhelming default intent is "go live". The dialog makes every user pay a tax on every live session to serve a minority use case. It also adds a step at the exact moment I am most impatient.

**Likely reason:** A catch-up feature was added and the team avoided choosing a default. The dialog is a safe choice for the team, not for the user.

### 4. FanZone keeps popping up

**What happens:** The FanZone panel keeps surfacing and takes focus from the game, even though I have not engaged with it.

**Why it matters:** An engagement feature that interrupts the thing people came for erodes the core experience. It also teaches users to dismiss prompts on reflex, which makes future features harder to introduce.

**Likely reason:** FanZone probably has its own engagement targets, and pushing exposure is the fastest way to hit them.

### 5. Cancellation only through a chatbot

**What happens:** There is no cancel button. To end the subscription, you have to go through a support chatbot.

**Why it matters:** This is a retention dark pattern. It may lower cancellations in the short term, but it burns trust at the exact moment a seasonal subscriber decides whether they will come back next season. That frustration shows up everywhere public reviews live: looping chatbots, repeated retention offers, and a 30-day notice period that often triggers one more charge after someone has already cancelled. Many European markets are also moving toward "cancel as easily as you signed up" rules; Germany, for example, has required a cancellation button for online subscriptions since 2022.

**Likely reason:** Churn is measured as a number to minimize, not as a relationship to manage.

### 6. Ads on a paid subscription

**What happens:** Despite paying ~€20 a month, I get an ad when I launch a game. It makes me question why I am paying if I still sit through commercials.

**Why it matters:** Paying subscribers on Reddit and the Play Store describe the same thing — 15–30 second unskippable ads before a game, and again every time they switch matches — and many name the ads as why they cancelled ([example](https://www.reddit.com/r/dazn_ca/comments/17mic1g/commercials_lead_to_cancel/)). The Android app's store rating looks fine in the US (~4.5★), but drops to roughly **3.2★ in Spain** and **3.6★ in Germany**, two of DAZN's biggest football markets. Across ~63,000 written reviews, the average sits closer to **2.2★**, with ads, loading, and cancellation as recurring themes ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/dazn-stream-live-sports/1129523589)).

**Likely reason:** Rights costs create pressure to layer ad revenue on top of subscriptions. That may help the P&L, but it leaves a paying fan feeling double-charged.

## Product principles DAZN should hold itself to

1. **My team first.** The product should know who I support and organize itself around that.
2. **Live means now.** Getting from opening the app to watching live should be as short as possible. No dialogs, no stale screens.
3. **Engagement is earned, not forced.** Social and fan features belong around the game, never on top of it.
4. **Leaving should be as easy as joining.** Seasonal fans will come back if they leave on good terms.

## If I were the PM

In priority order:

1. **Team onboarding and a team-first home.** Ask for favorite teams and sports at signup, with an option to edit in settings. Rank home as: my team live → my team upcoming → my league → everything else. Pin a "your team is live" hero card when a match is on.
2. **Default to live, with catch-up as a secondary action.** Start live playback immediately for live events, and put "watch from beginning" as a button in the player. No blocking dialog.
3. **Fix perceived live latency.** Cache the last known live state and render it right away, then refresh it. Use a clear skeleton for the live rail instead of stale content. Put a performance budget on how long the live rail takes to appear.
4. **Make FanZone opt-in.** Show it as a tab or side panel the user opens, remember dismissals, and never auto-expand it over playback.
5. **Add a cancel button in account settings.** Offer a pause or downgrade option and a single retention offer on the way out, and keep the chatbot for help, not as a gate.

## How I would measure success

- **Time to live playback** from app launch, especially in the 15 minutes around kickoff.
- **Share of sessions that reach my team's content** without searching.
- **Dialog removal impact:** live playback starts per session before and after the change.
- **FanZone opt-in rate and retention** among users who choose it, instead of raw impressions.
- **Win-back rate** among cancelled users next season, measured next to churn rate so trust is visible in the numbers.

## Key takeaway

DAZN already won the hard part by securing the rights. Its job now is to get out of the way: know my team, get me into the live game immediately, and let me leave without a fight. For a seasonal product, how easily someone can leave today decides whether they come back next season.
